Quick commerce

Ten-minute delivery doesn't forgive a regional average

Dark stores hold days of cover, not weeks. Demand is concentrated into specific hours and specific pincodes. Plan them like a warehouse and you'll be simultaneously out of stock in Powai and overstocked in Andheri. LogicstIQ plans per city, per dark store, per time of day.

Why the warehouse model breaks here

Lead times in hours

Replenishment cycles are measured in hours, not weeks, so a stockout can't be absorbed by safety stock sized for a monthly PO. Reorder points have to be recalculated against a short, volatile lead time.

Variability doesn't average out

One dark store serves a few square kilometres. Rain, a local festival or a nearby office holiday moves demand 40% in a day. Pool that across a city and the signal you needed disappears.

Fill rate is the scorecard

Platforms penalise short supply directly and delist quietly. Reorder points get set against your target fill rate, not a generic 95% service level borrowed from retail.

What you get back

  1. A reorder point per SKU, per dark store

    Not per city and not per region. Each store gets its own number, sized to its own demand distribution and its own lead time.

  2. Time-of-day demand shape

    Morning grocery, evening snacking and late-night convenience are different demand curves. Replenishment windows are planned against the shape, not the daily total.

  3. Stores ranked by stockout risk

    P90 stockout probability per store, so a limited delivery slot goes where the loss would be largest instead of where the route is easiest.

  4. Contribution per store

    Quick-commerce margins are thin and platform fees vary. The same RTO-and-fee engine that runs on marketplace SKUs runs here, so you can see which stores are worth serving. How that's calculated →

  5. A dispatch-ready purchase plan

    Per-store quantities aggregated into a single supplier PO and carton manifest, so the plan survives contact with your warehouse team.

Honest limitation. We plan; we don't integrate into platform vendor portals for automatic PO submission. You upload an offtake export and take the plan back to the portal yourself. For most suppliers that's a few minutes a week, and it's why this is free.

Questions

Why can't I plan quick commerce like a warehouse?

A warehouse aggregates demand across a whole region, so variability averages out. A dark store serves a few square kilometres, holds days of cover rather than weeks, and concentrates demand into specific hours. Averaging across stores hides exactly the volatility you need to plan for.

What data do I need to start?

A sales or offtake export with store or city identifiers, dates and quantities. Blinkit, Zepto, Swiggy Instamart, BigBasket, Flipkart Minutes and Amazon Fresh exports all work, as does any CSV or Excel file up to 25 MB.

How many stores can it handle?

There's no store limit on the upload. Very large files are summarised before the AI writes its explanation, but every store still gets its own computed reorder point.

Does it work alongside my existing WMS?

Yes, and that's the intended setup. We produce the plan; your WMS or the platform's own systems execute it. Nothing here needs to replace what you already run.
